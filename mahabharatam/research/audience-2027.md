# Audience 2027 · what is new, where viewers are heading, what to change in episodes 41–50

Researched 11 November 2026. Companion to `research/engagement.md`; nothing from that file is repeated here.

**Bottom line.** The ground has moved in our favour on story and against us on trust. Both platforms now build features for serialized short stories (YouTube launched "Shorts series" with seasons and episodes in September 2026; India's micro-drama audience is found almost entirely through social feeds), so an honest, well-told serial is the format of the moment. But 2026 was also the year of the "AI slop" purge: YouTube terminated and demonetized synthetic-voice, templated channels, surveys show viewers want AI labelled and trust unlabelled AI less, and India now requires platforms to collect a declaration for synthetic content. We are a faceless channel with synthetic voices, so our risk is being mistaken for slop by a classifier or by a viewer. The answer is not more stimulation. It is to say plainly what is synthetic, to show the human work and the source, to make every episode stand on its own for a viewer who arrives cold, and to be ready in Hindi, Telugu and Tamil. Evidence that any single visual effect raises retention is weak; the evidence for clarity, captions, labelling and a path to the next episode is much stronger.

**Labels.** **solid** = platform statement or study. **reported** = reputable press or a named industry report. **speculative** = forecast, opinion or my own reading of a rule.

**Limits of this research.** This session could search the web but could not open most pages (support.google.com, ppc.land and mondaq.com were blocked by the network allowlist). Claims below rest on search-result summaries of the linked pages, not on a full reading. Where a claim matters for compliance (section 1.1), check the primary page before launch. Figures from different reports use different definitions and must not be added together.

## 1. What is new on the platforms (2026)

### 1.1 AI labelling and disclosure: what we must label

Our case: code-drawn, clearly non-photorealistic animation; synthetic (TTS) voices that sound like people but clone no real person.

| Where | Rule | What it means for us | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **YouTube** | Creators must disclose altered or synthetic content that is **realistic**. Clearly unrealistic content, animation, special effects and AI used for production help are exempt. YouTube may add a label itself. | The frames are exempt. | **YouTube** (May 2026) | Labels moved to more visible spots; YouTube auto-labels some undisclosed AI videos. For unrealistic or animated content the label reportedly sits in the expanded description only. ## 2. Where audience behaviour and psychology are heading (2027+)

### 2.1 Reaction to AI-made content

- **solid (surveys; read via secondary summaries)** Meltwater/YouGov, April 2026, about 10,000 people in seven markets: 32 % would trust a brand less if its content were AI-generated, 15 % more. Clutch, Aug 2026, 601 US consumers: nearly 90 % want AI use disclosed; 42 % view AI positively for *educational* content. Fractl, Q2 2026, 1,008 US consumers: 91 % want AI video labelled, 87 % AI audio. These are US/global brand surveys, not Indian viewers of devotional content. https://letsdatascience.com/news/clutch-reports-consumer-distrust-of-ai-social-content-ea7c1666 · https://briefglance.com/companies/meltwater/pulses/26794
- **reported** India, 2026: police complaints over AI videos of Lord Jagannath (Jan), an FIR over AI videos of deities (June), a complaint over the "God in car" AI meme; the AI-made *Mahabharat* streaming series drew a backlash. BOOM found the most viral AI history clips rely on fabrication. https://www.deccanherald.com/india/odisha/fake-ai-generated-videos-of-lord-jagannath-surface-police-complaint-filed-3867751 · https://www.boomlive.in/decode/ai-history-videos-india-reels-youtube-viral-29514
- **reported** Meta–Ormax: 47 % of Indian micro-drama viewers find AI-generated content "creative". Acceptance is not uniformly negative. https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/are-micro-dramas-the-next-big-thing-in-india-47-find-ai-generated-content-creative-says-report-521234-2026-03-18
- **speculative (my reading)** By 2027 "is this AI?" will be a viewer's first question about any faceless channel. Three things earn trust: saying what is synthetic before being asked; a checkable source (our CE chapter references); a visible human who answers. Viewers punish being fooled more than they punish the tool. For sacred subjects in India the tolerance for glitches or invented scenes is near zero, which makes our fidelity rule a real advantage, provided we show it.

### 2.2 Appetite for serialized short stories

- **solid (Meta–Ormax, 2,000 interviews in 14 states, Nov 2025–Jan 2026)** 89 % of Indian viewers discover micro-dramas through social feeds, mostly without searching; 65 % found the format in the last year; median 3.5 hours a week in seven or eight short sessions a day; Tamil, Telugu and Kannada are the key languages after Hindi and English. https://about.fb.com/news/2026/03/social-feeds-are-powering-indias-micro-drama-boom-meta-ormax-report/
- **reported** Market estimates: about $260–300 M in India in 2025–26, forecasts of $1 B or more by FY30. Forecasts differ widely; treat as direction only. https://www.bdo.in/en-gb/news/2026/india%E2%80%99s-microdrama-market-could-reach-500-million-viewers,-favour-scaled-and-volume-driven-players
- **reported** Ormax: rural India supplies about 55 % of micro-drama viewers. https://www.ormaxmedia.com/stories/micro-dramas-the-india-story.html
- **speculative** The habit of following a story in one-minute vertical episodes is now mainstream in India and platforms are building for it. The micro-drama genre itself runs on paywalled cliffhangers and melodrama; we borrow the habit, not the manipulation. No data was found on mythology as a micro-drama genre.

### 2.3 Fatigue, "slow" content, long-form

- **reported** YPulse 2026 (ages 13–39, US/Canada): long-form video beat short-form overall for the first time in three years. Harris Poll, March 2026: Gen Z stepping back from most social platforms. https://www.ypulse.com/report/2026/09/10/media-consumption-report-15/ · https://www.hypebot.com/gen-z-is-still-on-tiktok-but-they-no-longer-trust-it-new-harris-poll
- **speculative** A "calm / cozy / slow content" turn is described in 2026 trend reports (Hootsuite, cited second-hand) and many blogs. No platform data on pacing was found; a claim that session time fell 12 % has no primary source. Do not treat as fact.
- **Meaning for us (speculative):** our measured pace (≤ 2.6 words/s, a real silence, a dynamic range of 4–6 LU) already sits on the calm side. The evidence gives no reason to speed up, and some reason to keep the long-form path in the launch plan (section 8) warm.

### 2.4 Captions, sound-off, languages

- **solid (XR/Extreme Reach survey, May 2026, 3,000+ people, US and Europe)** 80 % use captions at least sometimes; nearly 60 % of 18–24-year-olds use them always or often; 42 % of US viewers prefer captions even with perfect audio. The often-quoted "92 % watch on mute" is a 2019 figure; do not use it. https://agilebrandguide.com/captions-are-no-longer-optional-u-s-audiences-now-expect-them-everywhere-they-watch-video/
- **solid (IAMAI–Kantar)** 958 M active internet users in India (2025 edition), 57 % rural; the 2024 edition reported 98 % access the internet in Indic languages and 57 % of urban users prefer Indic-language content. https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/indias-internet-user-base-tops-950-million-as-rural-growth-ai-drive-adoption-513353-2026-01-29
- **Meaning (speculative):** English-only narration reaches the diaspora and urban English speakers, a minority of the Indian audience for this subject. Both platforms now dub automatically, but an auto-dub of sacred names is a risk we do not control. Because our voices are synthetic and our frames are code, a native re-render in Hindi and Telugu is cheaper for us than for almost anyone.

- **speculative** With Shorts on TV and family co-viewing, an episode may be watched by a grandparent and a child together, with comments visible beside it. That favours large type, clear speech, no gore held on screen, and a comment section we would be glad to have read aloud.

## 3. Storytelling craft for a serial

Honest state of the evidence: almost everything published on "what holds viewers in short serials" is industry opinion. The few controlled studies are small and not about 60-second video.

- **solid (lab, N = 133)** Wirz et al. 2023: cliffhangers raised arousal but did **not** increase enjoyment or the intention to keep watching. https://sonar.ch/documents/319990/files/2022_Wirz_Role.pdf
- **solid (two studies, about 500 readers, written stories)** Hahn et al. 2023: readers of a cliffhanger ending wanted the next instalment more than readers of a resolved ending; enjoyment and suspense did not differ. https://www.buffalo.edu/ubnow/stories/2023/06/hahn-cliffhangers.html
- **Reading the two together (speculative):** an open ending creates the wish for the next part but not satisfaction with this one. A serial that only withholds makes viewers want more and like it less. So each episode should close one thing and open one thing, and the next episode should answer early.
- **solid (YouTube figure)** 40 % of micro-drama views come 30+ days after release, and **solid (Meta–Ormax)** 89 % of discovery is by feed. Most viewers meet the series at a random episode, late. An episode that needs the previous one loses them; an episode that works alone and then offers the next one keeps them.
- **reported (weak)** Research on parasocial ties and character identification links attachment to characters with engagement, but no study found shows it predicts continued viewing of short serials. No study of recaps was found.
- **reported (industry, unsourced)** "An emotional beat every 15–20 seconds" is a micro-drama rule of thumb with no published data. A 2023 Peking University finding of higher arousal in micro-drama viewers is cited second-hand and could not be located.
- **reported** Indian micro-drama makers adjust later plot points to comments. We cannot change the plot, but we can let comments choose what we explain: which name, which term, which "the text says / tradition adds" note.
- **Craft implications (speculative, consistent with the above):** (1) one question per episode, asked in the hook and answered in the episode; (2) a second, larger question left open at the end that the text itself raises; (3) the answer in the first 15 s of the next episode; (4) a small recurring cast the viewer can recognise at a glance; (5) one felt moment per episode, given time and silence, not five.

## 4. Visual trends for illustrated shorts (Canvas2D / WebGL only)

Honest state of the evidence: no study was found that ties parallax, particles, lighting or kinetic type to retention in short video. What follows is perception research plus design-trend opinion.

- **solid (lab, eye tracking)** Faces draw fixations; eyes for anger and sadness, mouth for joy; with moving faces gaze stays at the centre of the face. A 2026 lecture study found a smiling presenter kept viewers looking at the content longer, with mixed results elsewhere. None of this is about retention in Shorts. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6242984/
- **solid (small, vendor-published)** An iMotions eye-tracking study of short-form formats found format changes moved attention and emotion measures but not recall. A caution: busier is not better remembered. https://imotions.com/blog/publications/short-form-videos-an-exploratory-study-on-the-impact-of-subtitles-and-asmr-split-screen-format-options-using-eyegaze-and-facial-expression-data/
- **speculative (trend reports)** 2026 design roundups agree on a reaction against AI polish: visible hand marks, grain, pigment and paper texture, intentional imperfection, lowered frame rates on some elements ("anti-AI crafting", attributed to Landor). The same roundups also push glossy "liquid glass", so the trend is not one-directional. https://www.creativebloq.com/design/graphic-design/texture-warmth-and-tactile-rebellion-the-big-graphic-design-trends-for-2026 · https://designity.com/blog/2d-animation-trends-2026
- **What this suggests for a mural style (speculative), all doable in our renderer:**
  - *Texture that reads as hand-made:* lime-plaster grain, pigment mottling inside fills, slightly uneven line weight, a faint craquelure. This is also what separates us at a glance from generative-video mythology.
  - *Depth by layers:* three or more planes with different drift, cooler and paler with distance, one moving light (lamp, dawn, fire) that changes the palette within a shot.
  - *Faces:* one close shot per episode where the eyes or brow change at the story's turn. A mural face can act with very little: eyelid, brow, tilt of head, a tear line.
  - *Atmosphere in service of the story:* smoke, embers, dust motes, rain, only where the text has them; kept off the caption band.
  - *Type in motion:* restrained. Names enter once, with a short identifier. No bouncing word-by-word "karaoke" captions; that style is the visual signature of the slop we must not resemble.
  - *TV legibility:* with Shorts watched on televisions, fine line work must not shimmer when scaled, and captions must be readable from across a room.

## 5. What to change in episodes 41–50

Twelve changes, ranked by expected effect within each group. Each keeps the promise: nothing is added to the text, nothing is teased that is not delivered. "Supports" points to the finding above.

### Do now, in the episodes

**1. Every episode stands alone for a cold viewer.**
Do: no line that needs the previous episode. Every proper name gets a 2–4 word identifier on first use in *each* episode ("Vasuki, king of serpents"), in voice or on screen.
Measure (script): for each proper noun, an identifier within the same or next caption chunk; zero phrases such as "as we saw" or "last time". Add to `tools/gate.mjs` next to the existing "≤ 2 new names per 10 s" check.
Supports: 89 % feed discovery (2.2); 40 % of views after 30 days (1.3).

**2. Close one question, open one, and answer it early next time.**
Do: the hook's question is answered inside the episode. The final image leaves a second question that the text itself raises. The next episode answers it in its first 15 s.
Measure (catalog + script): new fields `hook_answered_at_s`, `open_question`, and in the following episode `prev_answered_at_s ≤ 15`. Gate fails if any is empty.
Supports: Hahn (open endings raise desire for the next part) with Wirz (they do not raise enjoyment) (3).

**3. Captions carry the whole story and read on a TV.**
Do: full spoken text in captions; cap height at least about 3 % of frame height; strong contrast on the fixed band; no texture finer than 2 px that shimmers when scaled.
Measure (mp4): caption text equals narration text; glyph height ≥ 56 px on the 1920 frame; contrast ratio ≥ 7:1 sampled on the band; downscale to 540×960 and upscale to 2160 tall, then compare frame difference in static areas against a threshold.
Supports: XR caption survey (2.4); Shorts on TV (1.3).

**4. Say on screen what the source is and what is synthetic.**
Do: a small, quiet line on the closing card or a corner mark: the CE reference ("Adi Parva 1.45–46 · BORI Critical Edition") and "Voices: synthetic · Drawn in code". Same wording every episode.
Measure (mp4): OCR of the closing card finds both strings; on screen ≥ 1.0 s.
Supports: disclosure rules (1.1); trust surveys and India backlash (2.1); Mosseri (1.4).

**5. Build for other languages from episode 41.**
Do: move every on-screen string (hook text, captions, name tags, cards) into a per-language strings file; no English lettering painted into the art; leave room for longer Hindi/Telugu lines; keep narration timing data so a second voice track can be fitted.
Measure (pipeline): render one episode with a dummy `hi` strings file; zero Latin glyphs outside an allowlist; zero text overflow past the safe zone.
Supports: Indic-language preference (2.4); micro-drama languages (2.2); platform dubbing (1.3, 1.4).

**6. One face, close, changing at the turn.**
Do: at the 40–60 % turn, one shot where a face fills at least a third of the frame width and an expression changes (eyelid, brow, head tilt) during the existing silence.
Measure (mp4 + scene data): scene file declares `closeup: true` in the turn window; face bounding box ≥ 360 px wide; at least one expression keyframe change within it.
Supports: face-attention research (4); "one felt moment" (3). Evidence for retention is weak; treat as a craft bet and watch the retention curve at the turn.

**7. A recognisable recurring cast.**
Do: fix a signature per recurring character (two colours, one ornament, one silhouette trait) in the character bible and never vary it; show a name tag on first appearance in each episode.
Measure (scene data): palette and ornament of each character match the bible entry; name tag present at first appearance.
Supports: cold arrivals (1.3, 2.2); character attachment (3, weak).

**8. Hand-made texture and layered depth, never glossy.**
Do: plaster grain and pigment variation in fills, slightly uneven line weight, three or more depth planes with paler distance, one moving light per scene; particles only where the text has smoke, dust, rain or embers, and never over the caption band.
Measure (mp4): grain present (noise standard deviation in static flat areas within a set range, not zero); at least three layers with different horizontal drift in pans; particle pixels inside the caption band = 0.
Supports: anti-AI-polish trend and slop distrust (4, 2.1). Speculative; lowest-ranked of the episode changes.

### Do at launch, on the platforms

**9. Label truthfully on each platform.**
Do: Instagram and Facebook: turn the AI label on for every Reel (synthetic, realistic-sounding voice). YouTube: answer the altered-content question as its wording requires on the day, and in any case put "Voices are synthetic; every frame is drawn in code; text follows the BORI Critical Edition" in the description and the channel About. India: declare the synthetic voice wherever the upload flow asks.
Measure: a per-upload checklist line in `PERFORMANCE_LOG.md`; zero platform-applied labels that we did not apply first.
Supports: 1.1. Re-read the three primary pages in the week before launch; this session could not open them.

**10. Show the human and the making.**
Do: the intro in the owner's own voice (already optional in the launch plan; make it firm); one "how this episode was drawn" post per arc; a named person on the About page; replies in the first hour.
Measure: one making-of per arc; About page names a person; share of top-level comments answered within 24 h.
Supports: inauthentic-content enforcement against faceless synthetic-voice channels (1.2); trust moving to people (1.4, 2.1).

**11. Make the series a series, and make it findable later.**
Do: seasons and arcs as playlists from day one, in order; switch to Shorts series the day the channel is eligible; keep the forward links from `engagement.md`; titles that still make sense to a searcher in six months (a name or event plus "Mahabharata", question-first where true).
Measure: every episode is in exactly one ordered arc playlist; title contains a searchable name and the word "Mahabharata"; after launch, track the share of episode N viewers who start N+1 and the share of views arriving after day 30.
Supports: Shorts series and the 30-day tail (1.3); "Your Algorithm" topic clarity (1.4).

**12. Launch a second language once the first two weeks of data are in.**
Do: render Hindi and Telugu versions natively from the strings file with reviewed pronunciation of names, either as separate language channels/accounts or as extra audio tracks where the platform allows. Do not rely on auto-dub for sacred names; review any auto-dub before leaving it on.
Measure: a native reviewer signs off names and terms per episode; compare average percent viewed and sends per reach by language.
Supports: 2.4, 2.2, 1.3, 1.4. Owner decision: which languages, and who reviews.

## 6. Do not do

- **Do not hide or blur the synthetic voice or the code-drawn frames.** An auto-applied label or a viewer's discovery costs more than our own disclosure.
- **Do not present a synthetic narrator as a real person**, give it a human name and biography, or clone a real voice. This moves us into the "AI persona" and impersonation rules.
- **Do not use paywall-style cliffhangers**: no cutting mid-sentence, no "Part 2 or you'll never know", no ending that the next episode does not answer.
