# Engagement research · Shorts and Reels (2025–2026)

What gets serialized mythology and history Shorts watched and shared, and what to change in *Mahabharatam — As Vyasa Told It*. Researched November 2026.

**Labels:** **[O]** official, meaning platform docs or a named executive on the record. **[D]** data-backed, meaning third-party analysis of real data, with the method named and not audited. **[F]** folk wisdom, meaning common creator advice with no published evidence. Where no figure is given, none exists that we could verify.

## 1. How the platforms rank now

**Instagram Reels**
- **[O]** Mosseri (January 2025) named the three signals that matter most: **watch time, likes and sends**. Creators are pointed to *average watch time*, *likes per reach* and *sends per reach*. Sends (DM shares) matter more for reaching **non-followers**, and likes matter more for followers.
- **[O]** Reels Insights has shown a **retention chart** and **skip rate** (the share of viewers who leave in the first 3 s) since about August 2025. Mosseri: "the first three seconds… are super important", and a hook does not need bait.
- **[O]** Originality: since 2024, reposts are replaced by the original and aggregators are removed from recommendations. In April and May 2026 this widened to judging an account's output over 30 days. Our content is original, so this works in our favour. Do not cross-post a copy that carries a watermark from another app.
- **[O]** Reels can run up to 3 min (January 2025). The profile grid is now taller than square, so covers need a safe crop.
- **[O]** Hashtags are capped at **5 per post or reel** (December 18, 2025). Mosseri has said hashtags are not a major reach lever.
- **[O]** "We do not throttle the reach of AI content just for being AI content." Labels are separate from ranking.

**YouTube Shorts**
- **[O]** YouTube decides who sees a Short mainly by **"viewed vs. swiped away"** (in Analytics) and **average view duration / percent viewed**. It does not publish thresholds.
- **[O]** Since March 31, 2025, the public view count includes every start and **replay**. *Engaged views* (the old count) still drive revenue and YPP eligibility. Loops therefore raise the public number, and nobody outside YouTube knows how much replays weigh in ranking.
- **[D]** Paddy Galloway's study of about 3.3 billion Shorts views found the best performers sit at about **70–90 % viewed**. Treat this as a benchmark, not a rule.
- **[O]** Shorts can run up to 3 min (October 2024). No official source says longer or shorter ranks better. For a 55 s story, the share of the video watched counts for more than raw length **[F/D, widely reported]**.
- **[O]** YouTube's "inauthentic content" policy (renamed July 2025) bars from monetization **mass-produced, near-identical** uploads, such as "narrated stories with only superficial differences". AI tools are allowed. Our per-episode animation and unique scripts are the defence. Avoid shipping lookalike episodes.
- **Sound [F]:** Original audio is expected for story content. Trending sounds help dance and meme formats, not narrated drama. Neither platform claims a boost for original audio.

## 2. The first 1–3 seconds

- **[O]** Skip rate (Instagram) and swiped-away (YouTube) are measured in this window. Both platforms show it to you, which shows they care about it.
- **Patterns that fit honest story content [F, consistent across creator analyses]:**
  1. **Open in the middle of the action:** start on the strongest image of the episode, not on a title or a "Previously" card.
  2. **A concrete question a newcomer can follow, with a picture that matches it:** "Why did a son curse a king to die in seven days?" works better than "How did Takshaka kill Parikshit?", which needs two names the viewer may not know.
  3. **Stakes in the first sentence:** a number, a deadline or a vow ("seven days", "five hundred years a slave").
  4. **Movement in frame 1:** something is already moving (fire, smoke, a falling egg). A static first frame reads as a slideshow.
  5. **Text on screen that says the hook**, so viewers watching with the sound off are hooked too.
- **Avoid:** "You won't believe", fake shock, and teasing a payoff that never comes. That is clickbait, and it also raises late swipe-aways.

## 3. Holding attention across 55 s

- **Pattern interrupts [F]:** change the shot, scale or colour every **2–4 s**. Each line in our scripts already has a picture, which is right. Check that no single image stays on screen for more than about 4 s.
- **Mid-point turn [F]:** retention charts usually sag at 40–60 %. Put a reveal or a direct-speech line there, such as Shringin's curse, which is a good example.
- **Captions [O/F]:** both platforms auto-caption. Burned-in captions are standard for viewers watching with the sound off. Keep them inside the safe zone, at 2–5 words per chunk, with key names highlighted (as we already do).
- **Sound design [F]:** drones and fire crackle under the voice, a stinger (drum or bell) on each reveal, and a brief silence before the key line. Voice clarity comes first. TTS sounds more natural with varied pauses and pace.
- **Loops [O for counting, F for effect]:** replays now count as YouTube views. A smooth loop, where the last frame flows into the first (back to the fire), costs nothing.
- **Dead endings [F]:** a spoken "Next: <title>" plus a static card loses viewers in the last 3–5 s, and that lowers percent viewed.

## 4. Serialized-series specifics

- **[O] Instagram "Link a reel" (August 2025):** each Reel can link to the next one with a "Watch Part 2"-style button and a label of up to 15 characters. Link **Ep N to Ep N+1** in both directions where the feature allows.
- **[O] YouTube "Related video" link on a Short (set in desktop Studio):** YouTube names "connecting episodes of a series" as a use. Point Ep N at Ep N+1, and until N+1 is out, at the arc playlist.
- **[O] Playlists** (and Shorts series and shows, where available) give viewers a "next episode" path. YouTube does not promise extra reach for them.
- **Numbering [F]:** "Ep 7" in the title helps a returning viewer and sets up binge-watching. Lead with the story, though, because numbers mean nothing to cold viewers. Our template already does this.
- **Recaps [F]:** a recap is for followers, but most Shorts views come from non-followers. Put it **after** the hook, never before.
- **Cliffhangers [F, strong consensus]:** end on a question or an image, not on a summary. Ours do this. Keep the "next" beat short.
- **Pinned comment [F]:** "Ep 8 → <link> · Every episode in order → <playlist>". Links in Shorts comments are not clickable in every client, so this is a fallback, not the main path.
- **Trial Reels [O]:** a Trial Reel is shown only to **non-followers**. Results arrive in about 24 h, and it can auto-share to followers if it beats a threshold within 72 h. Use it to A/B test two hooks or covers for the same episode, then publish the winner.
- **Carousels [F]:** a "Family tree so far" or "Who's who in Season 1" carousel is easy to save and to send. It is a good companion between arcs.

## 5. Packaging

- **Covers:** pick the cover frame. Keep the key text in the **centre 3:4** for the Instagram grid. A consistent template (mural border, episode number, 2–4 words) makes the grid read as a series **[F]**.
- **Titles:** our "{title} · Mahabharatam Ep {n}" is clean. Test the question-first variant on YouTube, because question titles match search intent ("why did Shiva drink poison") **[F]**.
- **Captions:** Instagram truncates after about 125 characters, so line 1 is the hook **[O for truncation; F for effect]**. Searchable words (names, "Mahabharata", "story of") help Instagram and YouTube search **[F]**.
- **Hashtags:** use 3–5 relevant ones. YouTube ignores all hashtags on a video with more than 60 **[O]**. Instagram's cap is 5 **[O]**.
- **Cadence and time:** consistency beats volume. Neither platform publishes a best posting time. Use your own Insights and Analytics ("When your viewers are on YouTube") once you have data **[O for the tool]**. For an Indian audience, evenings IST are the usual folk advice **[F]**. Daily episodes within an arc fit what serial viewers expect.
- **Comment prompts:** ask a real question about the story or dharma ("Was Shringin right?"), not "comment 🔥 for Part 2". Instagram and Meta treat engagement bait as spam **[O]**. Do not reward false claims in comments, and correct them gently.

## 6. Devotional and mythology audiences

**What they respond to [F, observed across leading mythology channels]:** reverence without preaching. Moral dilemmas ("who was right?"). Little-known tales from a text they think they know. Clear source citations. Beautiful depictions of deities. Regional names and pronunciations done properly. Debate in the comments is engagement, not a problem.

**What they find offensive:** jokes about deities, sexualised gods or goddesses, and memes over sacred moments. Footwear or disrespect near murtis. Mispronounced divine names. "Myth" used to mean "false". Claims that contradict popular tradition **without saying where the claim comes from**. Comparisons that rank one faith above another. Glitchy AI deity imagery: AI-made religious content drew police complaints and a public backlash in India in 2025–26, including against an AI-made *Mahabharat* streaming series **[O, news reports]**. Note that our CE fidelity will sometimes contradict TV-serial memory (for example, Draupadi's prayer to Krishna). Mark it "As tradition tells it" or explain it with care, and never sneer at the popular version.

## 7. Prioritized changes (all low-risk, all keep the essence)

1. **Cold-open on the hook, then the recap** *(script/edit)*: swap the order to HOOK (0–3 s) → title → RECAP strip (2 s) → STORY. *Effect:* lower skip rate and swipe-away, because 0–3 s now earns the watch.
2. **First frame already moving, with the strongest image** *(visual/edit)*: start on the hook image (the hood in the smoke, the cracked egg) in motion, not on a card. *Effect:* lower skip rate.
3. **Hook questions a newcomer can follow** *(script)*: no unfamiliar names in the hook, plus a stake ("Why did a boy curse a king to die in seven days?"). *Effect:* a better viewed rate with non-followers.
4. **Hook text on screen from frame 1** *(visual)*: large Cinzel text with the question. *Effect:* holds viewers with the sound off.
5. **Shorten the NEXT tail** *(edit/audio)*: put "Next: <title>" on the card only (no voice), keep the card to 1.5 s or less, and end on the cliffhanger image. *Effect:* higher percent viewed.
6. **Seamless loop back to the fire** *(visual/audio)*: the last frame matches the first frame's fire, and the crackle carries across. *Effect:* more replays, which count as YouTube views.
7. **A turn at 40–60 %** *(script)*: put a direct-speech line or a reveal at the midpoint of each episode. *Effect:* flattens the mid-video drop.
8. **No image held longer than about 4 s** *(visual/edit)*: check the timings and add a push-in or a silhouette change. *Effect:* steadier retention.
9. **Stingers and silence** *(audio)*: a 200–400 ms rest before reveal lines, plus a bell or drum hit on beats you already have. *Effect:* attention resets and a more human TTS rhythm.
10. **Link every episode forward** *(packaging)*: Instagram "Link a reel" and the YouTube "Related video" link to Ep N+1 (or the arc playlist). *Effect:* more series watching, more follows.
11. **Trial Reels to test hooks** *(packaging)*: for each new arc's first episode, post two hook or cover variants as Trial Reels and publish the winner. *Effect:* data-driven hooks at no reach cost.
12. **One cover template, centre-safe** *(packaging)*: mural border, "Ep N", a 2–4 word title, with the text inside the central 3:4 area. *Effect:* a coherent grid and higher click-through from the profile.
13. **A real comment question in line 2 of the caption and in the pinned comment** *(packaging)*: a dharma question plus a link to the next episode. *Effect:* comments and session continuation. No bait.
14. **"Who's who" carousel after each arc** *(packaging)*: a family tree with the CE refs. *Effect:* saves and sends, and an entry point for new viewers.
15. **Vary visual grammar per episode** *(visual)*: keep the style fixed but vary the composition, so episodes are not "superficially different". *Effect:* protects monetization under YouTube's inauthentic-content policy.
