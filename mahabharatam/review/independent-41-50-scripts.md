# Independent review · scripts 41–50 (story and fidelity)

Reviewer: independent critic, did not write the scripts. Date: 11 Nov 2026. Scope: `script/ep41.en.json` … `ep50.en.json`, `script/ARC-41-50.md`, catalog entries 36–52, continuity against episodes 1–10, 19, 25, 31, 35–40. Nothing was edited.

## Verdict

The ten scripts are careful and mostly faithful: I found no invented event and no plain factual error in the voiced lines. They are not yet "no flaw". One thing breaks the series' promise as worded (the frame shows Shaunaka asking for the Bhrigu tales *after* Astika, the reverse of the text, with nothing on screen saying so). Thirteen things weaken the storytelling, and most of them are the same fault: the writer, afraid of adding to the text, cut the one clause that makes a beat make sense (why the demon needs the fire's word, why "eat everything" is a curse, who cursed the snake-sage, that Pramadvara died, that Satyavati agreed, that the war left a ruined kingdom). The Bhrigu arc also ends without returning to Shaunaka, whose family it is, although the text hands the payoff over: Ruru and Pramadvara's son is Shunaka, Shaunaka's forefather. Episode 47 is the strongest script of the ten, but its opening contradicts the picture the viewer was left with in episode 40 unless it says "while the fire still burned". Every fix below is one line long.

So the evidence grades below are:

- **Confirmed (G)**: confirmed against Ganguli/vulgate text quoted in search results this session.
- **Memory (CE)**: matches my own recollection of the CE Sanskrit or of van Buitenen; not re-read this session. I quote remembered Sanskrit only as a pointer for whoever checks; treat every quoted pāda as unverified wording.
- **Doubtful / wrong**: stated per item.

Nobody on this project has yet checked these ten scripts against the CE itself. That remains open and should be closed before voicing.

### BLOCKER

#### B1 · Eps 40→41 and 46 · the frame shows a false order of telling, undisclosed

Lines: ep 40 (aired) "But Sauti had another family to speak of: Shaunaka's own." · ep 41 `ask` "Sauti, tell me of my own family." · ep 41 recap band "A boy stopped a king's sacrifice. Now Shaunaka asks for his own family's story." · ep 46 `home` "His father told him how a boy once saved the serpents from a king's fire."

What is wrong: In the text Shaunaka asks for the Bhargava line first (1.5) and the Ruru tale *ends by pointing forward* to the Astika story, which Shaunaka then asks for (1.13). The series tells Astika first. That is a defensible choice, and the catalog records it. But the screen does more than reorder: the recap band says in words "**Now** Shaunaka asks", which states as fact that the request came after the serpent tales. A viewer who opens the CE finds the opposite. The promise is "the text as it is"; the ARC note admits "it is not said on screen". The source line "Adi Parva 1.5–6" does not tell a lay viewer that 5 comes before 13–53.

Evidence and confidence: the CE order (Pauloma 1.4–12, Astika 1.13–53) is not in dispute; the catalog's own `reorder_note` says so. High.

Fix (no voiced line changes):
- Ep 41 recap band, replace with: **"In the text this tale comes first: Shaunaka asks for his own family's story."** (14 words, same length as now.)
- Ep 41 source line: **"Adi Parva 1.5–6 · told before the serpent tales · BORI Critical Edition"**.
- Keep `ask` as it is; it is a fair paraphrase of 1.5.1–3.

With those two strings the reorder is honest and I would downgrade this to closed.

### MAJOR

#### M1 · Eps 46→47 · the hand-off implies the telling came after the rescue

Lines: ep 46 `cliff` "Beside that same fire, the Mahabharata was about to be told." · ep 47 `hook` "Then the poet himself walked in." · ep 47 `sat` "King Janamejaya sat at his great sacrifice."

What is wrong: 46 says a boy "once saved the serpents from a king's fire" and then that at that fire the Mahabharata "was **about to be** told": rescue first, telling after. Episode 40 showed the fire sink to embers. Episode 47 then opens "Then …" on a sacrifice in full session. A returning viewer reads: the sacrifice ended, *then* Vyasa came. The text (and ep 40's own line, "in the pauses of that sacrifice") says he came while it was going on. The script's later "Between the rites" is right but too quiet to undo the first impression. For a cold viewer "Then" has nothing before it, and "the poet" is the poet of nothing yet.

Evidence: 1.54.1, Vyasa comes on hearing that Janamejaya is consecrated for the snake sacrifice (Memory (CE), high; the writer's own note agrees). Also from memory, 1.48 lists Vyasa among the sadasyas of the rite.

Rewrites:
- 46 `cliff`: **"And while that fire still burned, the Mahabharata was first told beside it."** (13 words; was 11.)
- 47 `hook`: **"The Mahabharata's own poet walked in."** (6 words; was 6.)
- 47 `sat`: **"King Janamejaya's snake sacrifice was still burning."** (7 words; was 7. Also names which sacrifice for a cold viewer.)

#### M2 · Ep 41 `hook` · "Fire!" will be heard as an alarm

Line: "Fire! Whose wife is this?"

What is wrong: spoken by TTS in a raised voice at 0 s, with no context, "Fire!" is the English cry of alarm. The first second of the arc risks a laugh or a wrong read. The vocative needs a word in front of it.

Rewrite: **"Tell me, Fire: whose wife is she?"** (7 words, within the 8-word rule.) Alternative: "Agni! Whose wife is this?" (but the name is then undefined in the hook).

#### M3 · Ep 41 `fire` / `truth` · why the demon needs the fire's word is never said

Lines: "He turned to the household fire: Agni, the witness." … "Slowly, softly, the fire told him who she was." … "The demon became a boar, seized her…"

What is wrong: the hook asks "whose wife is this?"; the answer line says only "who she was". A cold viewer cannot tell what the fire said or why that answer licenses the seizure; if the fire said "Bhrigu's wife", why does he take her? In the text the rakshasa wants to be sure this woman is the one he chose, and says he will carry her off if she is; Bhrigu later asks who made her known, because the rakshasa did not know her as his wife. The script keeps the vagueness for a good reason (see fidelity table: the CE probably gives Agni no speech), so fix the motive, not the answer.

Evidence: Memory (CE) of 1.5 (the rakshasa's speech) and 1.6 (Bhrigu's question). Medium-high.

Rewrite of `fire`: **"He had to be sure it was she. He asked the household fire: Agni, the witness."** (15 words; was 9.) Pay for it in `alone`: **"Puloma was with child. Bhrigu had gone to bathe."** (9; was 10) and `came`: **"A rakshasa came: a demon who had once chosen her."** (10; was 11).

#### M4 · Ep 43 `curse` / `truth` · the curse has no sting

Lines: "Then you shall eat everything!" · "I was asked. I told the truth. A lying witness ruins seven generations."

What is wrong: to a cold viewer fire already eats everything; nothing in the episode says why this is a punishment. The writer removed "pure and impure" as a gloss, but the text carries the point in Agni's own mouth: he is the mouth through which gods and ancestors eat; how can their mouth be all-devouring? Without it, Brahma's "whatever your flames burn will be made pure" answers a question nobody asked, and the hook ("Why does fire eat everything?") is answered at 9 s with nothing left to learn. This is why the episode reads as argument: the stakes are missing, so only the reasoning is left. It also wastes episode 41's plant ("You are the mouth of the gods").

Evidence: Memory (CE) of 1.7 (Agni's speech: gods and ancestors eat what is offered in me; I am their mouth; how shall I be all-eating). Ganguli has the same sense. Medium-high.

Rewrite of `truth`: **"I told the truth. I am the mouth of the gods: how can their mouth eat everything?"** (17 words; was 13.) "Seven generations" goes (it is also a simplification: the text says seven before and seven after). Recover the words in `dark`: **"No offering rose. The world suffered."** (6; was 7) and `went`: **"The sages went to the gods; the gods to Brahma."** (10; was 11).

#### M5 · Ep 43 `pure` · "Not all of you" is ambiguous to the ear

Line: "Not all of you will eat all things. And whatever your flames burn will be made pure."

What is wrong: spoken, "not all of you" means "not every one of you people". The sense wanted is "not the whole of you". It is the turn line of the episode and it will be misheard.

Rewrite: **"Only a part of you shall eat all things. And whatever your flames burn will be made pure."** (18 words; was 17.) Fidelity: the text says he will not be all-eating with his whole body (Memory (CE), 1.7; Ganguli agrees).

#### M6 · Ep 44 `bit` / `lay` · the death is never stated

Lines: "It bit her, and she fell." · "She lay as if asleep, more beautiful than ever."

What is wrong: the episode's whole event is that the bride dies, and no line says so. "Fell", "as if asleep", "the sages wept" leave a cold viewer (and a child) unsure; `loop.answeredBy` points at a line that does not answer. Episode 45 says "lay dead"; 44 must too. Also, "more beautiful than ever" without "dead" reads oddly.

Rewrite of `lay`: **"She lay dead, as if asleep, lovelier than ever."** (9 words; was 9.)

#### M7 · Ep 46 `was` / `grass` · who cursed him is carried only by a plaque

Lines: "I was a sage, Sahasrapat, cursed to be a snake until I met you." · "Once, in play, he had frightened a friend with a snake made of grass."

What is wrong: the voice never joins the two. "Frightened a friend" and "cursed" sit side by side with no cause between them; captions-only and sound-only viewers (the research's own point 3: captions carry the whole story) do not get that the friend did the cursing, nor the neatness of it (a false snake, so a harmless snake). The writer flagged this; it is a real gap.

Rewrite of `grass`: **"Once, as a joke, he scared a friend with a snake of grass. The friend cursed him."** (17 words; was 13.) Recover in `vow` + `found`: **"Ruru killed every snake he saw. He raised his staff over an old, harmless one."** (15; was 8 + 8 = 16), which also removes the weak "over an old one" and plants "harmless" for the plea.

#### M8 · Eps 45–46 · the arc about "Shaunaka's own family" never returns to Shaunaka

What is wrong: the arc opens on "Sauti, tell me of my own family" and episode 42 pays one instalment ("he is your ancestor"). After that Shaunaka and Sauti do not speak again in 43's story, 44, 45 or 46. The finale closes Ruru's story and then jumps to another fire. The text gives the closing beat: Ruru and Pramadvara's son was Shunaka, the forefather from whom Shaunaka is named. The woman brought back with half a life is the listener's own ancestress. That is the arc's emotional end and it is missing.

Evidence: **Confirmed (G)**: Ganguli, Pauloma section V, "Ruru begot on his wife Pramadvara a son called Sunaka … O Saunaka, thy great ancestor" (search result this session). Memory (CE) of the same genealogy in 1.5 and 1.8. High.

Rewrite, ep 45, replacing `vow` (was 11 words) with a frame line and a shorter vow:
- `sauti`: **"They were married. Shaunaka, their son Shunaka gave your family its name."** (12)
- narrator: **"But Ruru swore to kill every snake he saw."** (9)

Pay for the ten extra words in `dead`: **"Ruru's bride, Pramadvara, lay dead of a snake's bite."** (9; was 13) and `court` (see m3 below). If 45 cannot hold it, put the Sauti line in 46 immediately before `arcend` and cut `home` to "His father told him how a boy saved the serpents from a king's fire."

#### M9 · Ep 47 `cliff` · "he" has the wrong antecedent

Line: "And he began by telling the whole story in one breath." (after Sauti's "I was there, Shaunaka. I heard it told.")

What is wrong: the last male subject is Sauti; before him, Vyasa. The student who begins was named two lines back. By ear this is Sauti or Vyasa beginning.

Rewrite: **"And Vaishampayana began with the whole story in one breath."** (10 words; was 11.)

#### M10 · Ep 48 `war` · "And they won." is not how the text ends its own summary

Line: "They asked for their kingdom, and were refused. So came the war. And they won."

What is wrong: the summary in the text closes on the warrior order destroyed and a kingdom recovered in ruins. "And they won" turns that into a triumph and takes away the one emotional note in an episode that is otherwise a list. It also sets a tone for the war that the series will have to take back.

Evidence: **Confirmed (G)**: Ganguli, "exterminated the whole race of Kshatriyas and slew king Duryodhana, and obtained back their devastated kingdom"; a summary of CE 1.55 found this session gives "a depopulated kingdom". Memory (CE): the last verse has the kingdom "mostly emptied". High for the sense, unverified for the CE wording.

Rewrite: **"They asked for their kingdom, and were refused. So came the war. They won back a land laid waste."** (19 words; was 15.) Recover in `before`: **"Vyasa's student began: all of it, in brief."** (8; was 9) and `won`: **"A princess won, Draupadi. A kingdom built."** (7; was 8).

#### M11 · Ep 49 · nothing says why this girl matters

Lines: `name` "Her name was Satyavati." · `cliff` "She grew up rowing a ferry, until a great sage stepped into her boat."

What is wrong: episode 48 asked why the full story begins with a king, a fish and a ferry girl; 49's `loop.prevAnsweredBy` points at "It begins with a king, Uparichara…", which repeats the question, not answers it. A cold viewer watches a chain of marvels and is never told what it is for. The answer (she is the poet's mother, and the royal line runs through her) is true, is in the same chapter, and can be said without spoiling 50, whose hook already gives the birth away.

Rewrite of `name`: **"Her name was Satyavati: one day, mother of the poet himself."** (11 words; was 4.) Recover in `king`: **"It begins with King Uparichara, who rode a crystal chariot through the sky."** (13; was 14) and `fish`: **"A fish swallowed it: an apsara under a curse."** (9; was 12; "a nymph of heaven" then sits on the plaque).

#### M12 · Ep 50 `scent` / `born` · her agreement is cut

Lines: "You will remain a maiden. Now choose a gift." · "She asked that her smell of fish become a fragrance." · "That same day, on a river island, she bore a son."

What is wrong: as written, a powerful man desires a ferry girl, she objects twice, he removes each objection by power, and next she has a child. Nothing says she agreed. The text does: having received the boon she is glad and goes to him of her own accord. On a family screen the cut makes the scene harsher than the source, which is the opposite of the care intended.

Evidence: Ganguli (search this session) has the boon, the fog, the fragrance and "Gandhavati/Yojanagandha"; Memory (CE) of 1.57: "having got her boon, pleased, she went to union with the sage". Medium-high; the CE wording is unverified.

Rewrite of `scent`: **"She chose a fragrance for her smell of fish, and gladly agreed."** (12 words; was 10.)

Also for care, in 50 `ferry` use "a young woman" on the plaque or in the picture note so that she is unmistakably adult; the voiced "ferry girl" of 48–49 then does no harm.

#### M13 · Ep 42 · the turn is misplaced and the true turn line stumbles

Lines: `river` "Her tears became a river: Vadhusara, the bride's river." (claimed turn) · `who` "Who told the demon who you were?"

What is wrong: the river is a grace note; nothing changes direction on it. The story turns when a rescue becomes a hunt for someone to blame, at `who`, and that line has "who … who" in seven words, awkward by ear. Put the silence and the close-up there.

Rewrite of `who`: **"Who told that demon you were my wife?"** (8 words; was 7.) This also repairs 41's logic after the fact (he had not known her as Bhrigu's wife). Evidence: Memory (CE) of 1.6, Bhrigu's question. Medium.

### MINOR

**m1 · Ep 42 `river` · who names the river, and the gloss.** Brahma naming the river is **Confirmed (G)** this session: Ganguli section VI, "the Grandfather of the worlds seeing that river follow the path of his son's wife gave it a name himself, and he called it Vadhusara. And it passeth by the hermitage of Chyavana"; my memory of the CE (1.6) agrees. So the writer's reading stands and can be used. The plaque gloss "the bride's river" is loose: *vadhū* here is Brahma's daughter-in-law (Bhrigu is his son), and Puloma is a wife near her term, not a bride. Rewrite: **"Her tears became a river. Brahma himself named it: Vadhusara."** (10 words; was 9); plaque: "the daughter-in-law's stream". Bonus: Brahma is planted before he rules in 43.

**m2 · Ep 45 `court` · "Dharma, lord of the dead".** The text says Dharmaraja. "Dharma" alone will collide with episode 52 ("Vidura of Dharma") and with Yudhishthira's father. Rewrite: **"…to Dharmaraja, lord of the dead."** Plaque: "Dharmaraja · Yama, lord of the dead".

**m3 · Ep 45 `court` · "Her father, a king of the gandharvas".** Episode 44 gave her an apsara mother and a sage who raised her; no father was voiced or plaqued (44's `names` has no Vishvavasu, though its `source` says he is on a plaque). Rewrite: **"Her father by birth, a gandharva king, went with the messenger to Dharmaraja, lord of the dead."** (17; was 16), or drop him: "The messenger went to Dharmaraja, lord of the dead." The text does have both go (Memory (CE) 1.9; Ganguli agrees).

**m4 · Ep 45 `hook` and `vain` say the same thing.** The hook gives the offer; `vain` gives it again; the turn is then no surprise, and `loop.hookQuestion` ("What does it cost?") is answered by the hook itself. Rewrite of `vain`: **"The dead do not return. But the gods have left one way."** (12; was 13), and set the loop question to "Will he pay it, and will it work?" (answered by `woke`).

**m5 · Ep 46 `teach` · scope of the teaching.** In the text the rule is stated of all breathing things and then pressed on Ruru as a brahmin (the warrior's duty is said to differ). The script's universal form is the first half and is fair; do not add a plaque that claims more ("for everyone, always").

**m6 · Ep 46 `home` · the text's own time-knot.** Ruru is three generations below Bhrigu, his father tells him of Astika, and Sauti himself attended that sacrifice. The knot is the text's; "once" hides it well. Do not add dates or "long ago" to either side.

**m7 · Ep 36 (aired) and 46 · who taught Astika.** Ep 36 says "the sage Chyavana". Ganguli says Chyavana; a Sanskrit reference work found this session says Pramati, son of Chyavana; my memory of the CE verse is "from the Bhargava, Chyavana's son". **Unverified.** If the CE reads "Chyavana's son", ep 36 follows the vulgate, and 46 gains a true payoff for free: the father who tells Ruru the story of Astika was Astika's own teacher. Check 1.44 when the CE can be opened; until then change nothing.

**m8 · Ep 47–48 picture notes · palm leaves.** "Palm leaves under his arm", "lifts the bound palm leaves", "the manuscript's pages fan open". The text has an oral recital; the written epic was tagged as tradition in ep 4. A manuscript on screen quietly asserts what ep 4 took care to tag. Use breath, beads or an open hand.

**m9 · Ep 48 `before` · "began with a summary".** Flat, and it tells what the hook just said. Covered by the M10 rewrite.

**m10 · Ep 48 `here` · the famous line is unqualified.** The text limits it to the four ends of life (law, profit, pleasure, release). The short form is how it is always quoted and is acceptable; put the qualifier on the plaque: "on law, wealth, love and freedom".

**m11 · Ep 49 `boy` · "The king kept the boy."** No line brings the children to the king; the last subject was fishermen. Rewrite: **"They took them to the king. He kept the boy; the apsara, freed, rose to heaven."** (16; was 10) only if M11's savings allow; otherwise let the picture show the fishermen at the palace.

**m12 · Ep 50 `cliff` · the jump to episode 51.** "His poem is full of gods born as men. Why did they come down?" changes subject and narrator level with no bridge. In the text the king asks this next. Rewrite: **"Then the king asked: why were gods born on earth as men?"** (12; was 13.) Memory (CE) of 1.58's opening; medium.

**m13 · Ep 44 · the first half is a list.** Six lines of set-up (left, found, named, loved, set, play) before anything goes wrong; `set` ("Their fathers set the wedding day.") can go to the picture, and its time given to a held beat on `loved`. The episode is also at 2.57 words/s, the batch's fastest, in its tenderest story.

**m14 · Hook shapes.** 43, 46 and 49 are all "Why/What …?" over an answer image, every third episode; 47 and 50 both open "…the poet…". Within the rotation rule, but a viewer of the playlist will feel the pattern. M1's hook for 47 removes one repeat.

**m15 · Ep 43 `because` · "The fire, Agni, told a demon the truth, and a sage's wife was carried off."** Fine for a cold viewer, but it makes the fire sound guilty before Agni defends himself. "…the truth; the demon then carried off a sage's wife" keeps the cause without the blame.

**m16 · Pronunciation for TTS.** Needs checked overrides before voicing: Pramadvara, Sahasrapat, Vadhusara, Uparichara, Vaishampayana, Dvaipayana, Chyavana (not "chai-"), Dharmaraja. "Puloma/Puloman" is already handled by silence.

## Continuity with episodes 1–40

Sound: Sauti/Ugrashravas, Shaunaka as the elder, the Naimisha fire, Janamejaya's vow and the builder's warning, Agni's identifier, "in the pauses of that sacrifice", Vaishampayana as first reciter (ep 4), Chyavana as ancestor (36 → 42). Nested tellers are right in 47–48 (Vyasa → Vaishampayana → Janamejaya; Sauti → Shaunaka). Open points: the false order of the frame (B1); the sacrifice seen ending in 40 and running again in 47 (M1); ep 40's plant ("Shaunaka's own" family) is opened in 41, half paid in 42 and never closed (M8); 49–50 are told by the narrator with no sign that Vaishampayana is speaking to the king (m12 restores it in one line).

## Arc craft

- **41–46.** One family, four generations, three tales. 41–43 work as a unit; 44–46 work as a unit; the join (43's last line) is clean. The end is weak: the lit payoff belongs to a character met ninety seconds earlier, and the family whose story it is has left the room. M8 fixes that.
- **47.** Lands. The silent turn and "I was there" are the best beats in the batch. It needs M1 and M9 and nothing else.
- **48.** Works as a trailer only if it ends on cost, not victory (M10).
- **49–50.** Faithful and clear; 49 needs a reason to exist for a cold viewer (M11), 50 needs her agreement (M12). The hand-off to 51 is honest.
- **Pacing.** 43 and 46 carry the most ideas; both become easier with the rewrites, which trade argument for cause. 44 is the fastest-spoken and should be the slowest.

## Sensitivity

- **41, the abduction.** Handled with restraint; "seized her" is enough. Keep the boar's hold off her body in the drawing. No change to words.
- **42, the fall from the womb.** The text's own image; the script moves at once to the child shining. Fine.
- **44, a dead girl "more beautiful than ever".** The text's line; acceptable once "dead" is said plainly (M6).
- **49, "his seed", "smelled of fish", the daughter given away while the son is kept.** All the text's; said once, flatly. Fine. Do not add comment.
- **50.** See M12. With her agreement restored, and the union left to the mist, it is suitable for co-viewing.
- **Varna and sect.** Nothing mocking or sectarian. "A fisherman's daughter" is neutral. 46's teaching is given in its universal half (m5).
