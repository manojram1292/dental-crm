## The universal form (Bhishma 6.33 = Gita 11), element by element

What happens, in the constituted text: Arjuna asks to see the lordly form, "if you think I am able to see it" (3–4). Krishna tells
him to look, and gives him a divine eye, because his own cannot see it (5–8). Sanjaya: Hari showed him the form (9–13). Arjuna,
amazed, his hair on end, bows his head, joins his palms and speaks (14); what he says he sees is verses 15–31. He asks who this is
(31). The answer begins `kālo 'smi` (32–34). Arjuna, palms joined, trembling, answers (35–46); Krishna shows his own gentle form
again (47–50).

**Drawn, and not in the verses** (staging, marked so that nobody takes it for the text):

- the NUMBER of faces (twenty-three are drawn, most of them cut by the frame) and of arms (eighteen): the text says "many", "endless";
- the arrangement: one great blue face in the middle with rows of faces round it; a single blue torso with necklaces;
- the swords, spears and open hands among the raised weapons (the text names only the mace and the discus; verse 10 says "many
  weapons");
- the mark on each brow, the shape of the crowns, the red jewel;
- the chariot under Arjuna and its banner (from 6.23; the text of 6.33 does not say where he stands);
- the two armies drawn below the form, row behind row down to the bottom of the frame, an aisle between them, lit by the form
  (third cut: the lower part of the frame was empty). That warriors of BOTH sides enter the mouths is the text (26: Dhritarashtra's
  sons … `sahāsmadīyair api yodhamukhyaiḥ`, "with the chief warriors of our side too"); their ranks, banners and number are staging;
- a fourth row of faces at the top (third cut: the picture sits 150 px lower so that the horizon is low in the frame);
- that he half-kneels (the text has him bow his head and join his palms; later, 44, he lays his body down in prostration, which
  is not drawn);
- the rays turning in the sky; the camera's pull-back.

**In the verses, and not drawn**: the gods, Brahma on his lotus seat, the seers and the serpents seen in the body (15); the many
bellies, thighs and feet (16, 23); the heads crushed between the teeth (27c) and the licking of the worlds (30), left out as
restraint; the gods and seers praising with joined hands (21–22); the four-armed form he asks to see again (46) and the return
to the gentle human form (50–51).

**Star passages printed in this chapter and not used**: 0102 (after 27b: "like a thousand suns …", "ready to swallow the
world"), 0103, 0104, 0105, 0106, 0107. The "thousand suns" of this film is verse 12, which is constituted text.

## Cut because of the check, or left out because it was not verified

- **Krishna raising the wheel against Bhishma** (6.55.86–98, the first cut's opening): gone from this cut; the opening is now a
  title over the armies.
- **Arjuna winning Draupadi at the bow contest** (1.179): dropped for length (five arrows, not "one shot", stays noted).
- **Draupadi dragged by the hair** (2.60.22–28): the separate shot is dropped; the cloth has two shots.
- **Krishna supplying the cloth**: star passage (543*). Not shown, not said.
- **"Before the first arrow"**: an early draft of the line that returns to the chariot. Cut: 6.23.20 says the weapons had already
  begun to fly. (Second cut: "But first"; the third cut needs neither.)
- **The opening on the chariot and the three wordless returns to it** (second cut): gone. To a first-time viewer the repeated
  picture and the repeated words "Two armies" read as the film starting again. With them went "Between them, one archer will not
  fight" (6.24.9) and "But first".
- **The future tense** ("will fall", "will curse"): gone with the reordering; the war is now told after the universal form, in the
  present tense.
- **The night raid** (Sauptika 10.8.9–11, "A camp raided in its sleep"): read and verified for the second cut, voiced for this one,
  and left out for length: with the makers' lines and the held end card the film would have run past 90 s. The war is now three
  beats (Bhishma, the boy, Karna's wheel).
- **"In the forest,"** (the demon line): dropped for length only; the forest is in the picture (1.139.1).
- **"Told in order"**: see below.
- **Abhimanyu's age, the six warriors by name; Karna's curses; the dog as Dharma; Ekalavya; the hundred insults**: not read, not said.

## The promise

Spoken: "Every book. Every vow. Every war. From the first verse to the last. Nothing added for effect."

- "Every book": the catalog (SERIES.md) plans all eighteen parvans, Adi to Svargarohana; their names are on screen.
- "Every vow. Every war.": a promise of coverage, as the brief worded it. The series condenses (about a minute an episode); it
  does not skip the story. Nothing in the trailer says "nothing is missed" or "every verse".
- "From the first verse to the last": the series begins at Adi 1.1 and is planned to end with the last book.
- **"Told in order" was NOT used**, although the brief suggested it: SERIES.md records three places where the series moves
  material for continuity (Parikshit's death before Kadru and Vinata; the Bhrigu tales after Astika; Bhishma's ten days told in
  order rather than as Sanjaya's flashback). The trailer therefore does not promise the CE's order.
- "Nothing added for effect": the rule of this file.
