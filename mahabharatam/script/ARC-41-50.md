# Episodes 41–50 · the story plan

Phase A of the batch: the plan and the scripts, written before any episode is voiced or rendered, for an independent story critic to read. Scripts: `script/ep41.en.json` … `ep50.en.json` (drafted in `tools/write_scripts_41_50.py`). Every script passes the script gate (`node tools/gate.mjs --ep N`) on predicted voice lengths (`tools/estimate.py`: mean error 0.22 s a line on episodes 21–40, so a voiced episode can land about ±1.5 s from the figure given here).

Two arcs meet in this batch:

- **41–46 · Tales of the Bhrigus** (catalog S01A06, a side arc; CE Adi 1.5–12, the Pauloma sub-parva). Six episodes, one finale.
- **47–50 · Vyasa Begins** (catalog S01A07, the main line; CE Adi 1.53–57). The first four of its six episodes; it ends at episode 52.

## 1. Why the Bhrigu tales come here

In the Critical Edition the Pauloma sub-parva stands *before* the Astika sub-parva: Shaunaka asks Sauti to tell the Bhargava line first (1.5.1–3), and only then asks about the snake sacrifice. The series reordered this (catalog `reorder_note`): Astika first, the Bhrigus after, so the last Bhrigu tale (a man who hates snakes is taught mercy by one, and is told to hear how the serpents were saved) closes the serpent theme instead of interrupting it.

The reorder was planted twice and is paid off in episode 41's first story line:

- Episode 36, Sauti: "Shaunaka, that teacher was of your own family." (Chyavana, Astika's teacher.)
- Episode 40, last line: "But Sauti had another family to speak of: Shaunaka's own." (A woman and a boar in the fire's smoke.)
- Episode 41, 3.9 s, Shaunaka: "Sauti, tell me of my own family." Sauti: "Your line begins with the sage Bhrigu and his wife, Puloma." Episode 42, Sauti: "So he is called Chyavana, the fallen one. Shaunaka, he is your ancestor."

The arc is one family across four generations (Bhrigu, Chyavana, Pramati, Ruru), and one idea turned over three times: **what a true word costs**. Agni tells the truth and is cursed for it (41–43). Ruru gives half his life on his word (45). A curse spoken in fright binds a friend until the right man comes (46). The reorder is stated in each script's `source` and in the catalog; it is not said on screen (the series has never put the reorder on screen; the source line now names the chapters, which shows a viewer that 41 comes from chapter 5).

## 2. The finale rule (episode 46)

From the independent review of episodes 21–30, as applied in episode 40: the last episode of an arc holds a **lit payoff of at least 4 s** and then shows an **arc-end card**.

- Payoff: line `freed` ("The snake's shape fell away, and the sage stood in light."): the voice, then a 2.1 s hold on the sage's face in his own form, aqua-white on gold; about 5.5 s in all. It is also the episode's turn, after a real silence.
- Card: line `arcend`, 1.5 s, silent: THE END OF AN ARC · THE BHRIGUS · Episodes 41–46 · the playlist line (words in `strings`).
- The last line then hands on to the main story: "Beside that same fire, the Mahabharata was about to be told."

## 3. How 47–50 land the series' first big moment

Episode 1 promised "the story he heard at King Janamejaya's great sacrifice … composed by Vyasa". Forty-six episodes later the poet walks into that sacrifice.

- **47** is built as an arrival, not an explanation. The hook is the figure coming between the pillars with his face unseen; the signature shot is the same walk, near and lit; the turn is silent: asked how the war began, **Vyasa does not answer**, and the one close-up of the episode is his face in that silence as he turns to his student. Then the frame closes on itself: Sauti, "I was there, Shaunaka. I heard it told." (CE 1.1: he heard Vaishampayana tell it there.) The nested tellers are now all on screen: Vyasa composed it, Vaishampayana tells it to Janamejaya, Sauti heard that and tells it to Shaunaka, and the narrator tells us.
- **48** is the whole epic in one breath (CE 1.55), six panels in six colours, with the dice as the turn; then the king's "tell me all of it" and the line every reader waits for: what is here is found elsewhere; what is not here is nowhere (1.56).
- **49–50** begin the long telling where the text begins it (1.57): a king, a fish, a ferry girl, and the poet's own birth. Episode 50 ends by handing to 51 (why gods were born as men), not on Vyasa's promise to his mother, which is a long plant paid off much later (Adi 1.99) and must not be sold as a cliffhanger.
- From 47 the three frame figures are acting figures in their bible colours (`vyasaSage`, `vaishampayanaSage`, `kingJanamejaya`) so they can carry close-ups. Vaishampayana's voice was changed before its first use (it was Sauti's own voice raised; two nested narrators must not sound alike).

## 4. Rules every episode in this batch follows

Kept from episodes 31–40: hook ≤ 8 words and spoken by ≤ 3.0 s (aimed ≤ 2.9); story by 5 s; turn at 40–60 % after a real rest; ≤ 59 s; ≤ 2.6 words/s; ≤ 2 new names per 10 s; no undefined pronoun in the hook; one fixed caption band; a silent Next ≤ 1.2 s.

New (research/audience-2027.md §5, and the owner's brief), all checked by `tools/gate.mjs` on the script:

1. **Stands alone.** Every name has a 1–6 word identifier in the script's `names` map, to be shown as a plaque at first appearance. No line leans on an earlier episode. The recap band still says one line.
2. **Close one question, open one.** `loop.answeredBy` names the line that answers the hook's question; `loop.prevAnsweredBy` names the line that answers the previous episode's, and it must start by 15 s (here: 0–11 s; the hook's own picture shows the answer at 0 s in every episode).
3. **The source line** on the cliffhanger and the Next: "Adi Parva 1.x · BORI Critical Edition" and "Voices: synthetic · Drawn in code".
4. **Colour script.** Every line names a palette; no hue (within 30°) for more than two lines; at least two palettes the episode before did not use.
5. **Camera.** At least four different moves, one signature shot, at least two transitions born from the medium (ink, brush, lamp glow, smoke), one face close-up with a change of emotion.
6. **Sound.** An ambience per place, a foley cue on every spoken line, a music mood per section, at least two J-cuts, and two real silences where the story allows (every episode here has two).
7. **Hook styles rotate**; no two neighbours alike: speech, statement, question, fragments, speech, question, statement, number, question, statement.

## 8. Self-check of the scripts

- **Claims not in the cited chapters.** Changed from the catalog's plan or kept out of the scripts: Agni's spoken answer to the rakshasa (vulgate only; the catalog had him "admit she was first promised to the demon"); "promised" became "chosen her … her father had given her to Bhrigu"; Brahma naming the river (the CE as the writer reads it, unverified this session: not said, not shown); "pure and impure" in the curse (a gloss); Vyasa "rising to speak" (the catalog's old cliffhanger for 46: he does not, and he comes during the rite). Kept and flagged in the scripts' `source`: the dundubha as "an old snake"; "grew at once" (from 1.54, used in 50); "three years" (1.56, on a plaque in 48).
- **Lines a first-time viewer could not follow.** Episode 43's first story line restates the cause ("The fire, Agni, told a demon the truth, and a sage's wife was carried off"); 45 restates the death; 46 restates the vow; 47 names the king and the sacrifice; 50 restates who Satyavati is. The recap band adds one line in each.
- **Cliffhangers and their payment.** Each is answered by the next hook's picture at 0 s and in words by 4–11 s (the table in each episode below). Episode 50's last line needs episode 51 to open on the answer (the Earth, overburdened, goes to Brahma): a requirement on the next batch.
- **Telling what the picture should show.** Left to the picture and not said: the fruit Puloma offers her guest (41), Bhrigu's raised arm and the thrown water (42–43), the water for Vyasa's feet (47), the leaf being folded (49). Close to the picture but kept, because the action is the story: "The demon became a boar, seized her, and ran like the wind"; "The snake's shape fell away, and the sage stood in light".
- **Direct speech.** Every quoted line is a condensed paraphrase of speech the text gives to that speaker at that point; none is presented as a verse. Sauti's and Shaunaka's frame lines follow the series' practice (episodes 2–3, 36).
- **Respect.** Agni is drawn as a fire with a calm face, never comic. Dharma is a grave king, not a monster. The union in 50 and the wound in 44 are not shown. "His seed" (49) is the text's own word, said once.

## 9. What a critic should press on

- Episode 43 carries the most words (112 in 57 s) and two speeches of ideas; it may read as argument, not story.
- Episode 44 has thirteen short lines; the first half is exposition. The turn is at 57 %.
- Episode 46 is the most compressed: the snake's argument, the back-story, the release and the teaching in 57 s. The friend's curse is carried by picture and plaque, not by the voice.
- Episode 48 is fragments by design. Without the pictures the summary is a list.
- Episode 47's hook ("Then the poet himself walked in.") assumes a viewer cares who "the poet" is; the card and the halo have to do that work.
- Vyasa's design changes between the old module (episodes 4 and 40) and the acting figure (47 on): same colours, a new body.
